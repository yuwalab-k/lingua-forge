When the fishermen left, the bartender picked up their glasses and took them into the kitchen.
He put them in his dish washing machine, locked the handle, and turned on the machine.
Drippy was awakened by a loud rumble and a sudden blast of water as the dishwasher began to work.
All the dishes around him were rattling and shaking, and water was pouring over everything like a giant waterfall.
If this is what it's like to be drunk, I'll never do that again.
Everything was spinning around.
It was like being in the middle of an earthquake.
Drippy hid under a spoon.
I'm safe under this spoon.
And he was having such a fine adventure that he forgot all about going home to his family.
I must be the bravest raindrop who ever lived.
Wait until I tell my mother how I killed that shark and about the earthquake I was in.
And Drippy lay snugly under the spoon, thinking about how wonderful he was.
But suddenly, very suddenly, the hot air cycle came on.
Now in a dishwasher, first the water washes and rinses off the dishes, and then the blower comes on and dries them with very hot air.
That is good for the dishes, but it is very bad for raindrops.
Because hot air causes raindrops to melt away.
And that is exactly what happened to Drippy.
He started to feel himself melting.
He clung closer to the spoon.
But the spoon was getting hotter and hotter, and Drippy had to let it go.
He could feel himself getting smaller and smaller.
And he knew that in a moment, he was going to disappear forever.
