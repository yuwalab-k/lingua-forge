【DRIPPY DISC04】#03

Drippy quietly slipped out of the ant's nest and moved out into the roadway again.
He still had no idea how he was going to get out of this place and up to the ground.
He walked along the path until he came upon a strange looking creature with long horns and a hard shell slowly crawling along.
Hello, could I ask you a question?
The creature stopped.
I'm in no hurry.
What do you want to know?
I want to know how to get out of here.
The creature stared at him.
You're Drippy, the raindrop, aren't you?
Drippy was terribly surprised.
How did you know?
Everybody knows who you are.
You're the runaway.
You ran away from your mother and father and brothers and sisters and cousins.
Who are you?
I'm a snail.
Would you help me, Mr. Snail?
I suppose so.
I hope you're not in a hurry, little raindrop, because I move very slowly.
I don't mind.
All he wanted to do was get out of this place.
The reason I move slowly is because I'm a snail.
I understand, Drippy said, but he did not understand at all.
Get on my back.
I'm going upstairs.
I'll take you with me.
He waited for Drippy to say, thank you.
But instead, the little raindrop crawled up on the snail's back and said, I'm ready.
The younger generation has no manners, the old snail thought.
Hang on.
The snail was right.
He did move slowly.
In fact, he moved very slowly.
He was anxious to get above the ground, and he wondered whether they would ever reach there.
The snail began to climb upward, through the dirt, higher and higher, until suddenly they were out in the open, on the ground, back in the zoo.
This is the end of the road, the snail said.
You can get off now.
And again, he waited for Drippy to say, thank you.
But all Drippy said was, snails really do move slowly, don't they?
And he slid off the snail's back.
The snail sadly shook his head and slowly walked away.
Drippy was glad to be above the ground again.
He did not like it very much down below.
He wondered how all those millions of creatures could spend all their lives there without ever seeing the sunlight or the moon or the stars, without ever riding on the wind or on the back of a grasshopper.
Here above the earth, it was so different.
Drippy looked around him and saw hundreds of animals, and they were all strange looking.
There were zebras with black and white stripes, and giraffes with long necks, and yellow lions, and orange tigers, black panthers, and gray hippopotamuses.
And Drippy was proud that he could name all their colors.
I'll bet there isn't another raindrop in all the world that knows the names of all the colors.
I'm very special.
The elephant that had stepped on him before was coming toward him, and Drippy quickly got out of the way.
He did not want to go underground again.
