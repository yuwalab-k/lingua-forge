In the bedroom, the little girl was going to sleep too, but this time it was a healthy sleep and her mother and father knew that she was going to be all right.
They had no idea that they owed all this to a tiny little raindrop that had turned into a rainbow for a moment.
Mr. Harris said to his wife, Our daughter is going to be fine.
I have to go to work now.
I mustn't be late.
He kissed his wife goodbye, went into the room where the rabbits were, picked up the cage where Drippy was asleep in the rabbit's ear and took it out to his car.
A few minutes later, Mr. Harris drove off to go to work.
It was a short drive, but Drippy did not know that.
He was sound asleep in the rabbit's ear.
Mr. Harris drove to the front of a theater in the downtown section of the city.
There was a huge sign in front of the theater that read, Ten Acts of Audeville Starring Harris, The Greatest Magician on Earth.
Mr. Harris looked at the sign and drove around to the back of the theater.
He carefully lifted the rabbit's cage and carried it into the stage door.
The doorman greeted him.
Good day, Mr. Harris.
Good day, Sam.
Mr. Harris said.
Mr. Harris, carrying his cage, walked back to his dressing room.
Past acrobats and jugglers and dancing girls, they were all very good.
But Mr. Harris was the star of the show.
He truly was the greatest magician in the world.
He took the cage into his dressing room and changed into his magician clothes.
The show would begin in a few minutes.
His assistant came in and said, Shall I put the rabbit near the stage for you?
